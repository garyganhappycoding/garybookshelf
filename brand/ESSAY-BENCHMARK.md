## The paper at a glance
| Part | Task | Length | CEFR target | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short communicative message (e.g. email to a friend) | about 80 words | A2 | 20 |
| 2 | Guided writing, an **essay** with given content points | 125-150 words | B1 | 20 |
| 3 | Extended writing, choose ONE of 3: **story**, **report** or **article** | about 200-250 words | B2 | 20 |
Paper 2 is 60 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes. Write the question number of the chosen Part 3 task.

## The four criteria (same in every part)
| Criterion | What the examiner asks | HIGH (band 5) | AVERAGE (band 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content** | Did you answer every content point, relevantly? | ALL points covered, all relevant, **reader fully informed** | Points mostly covered; **minor irrelevance or an omission**; reader informed **on the whole** |
| **Communicative achievement** | Right genre, tone, register, format; ideas clear? | Uses the conventions of the task **effectively to hold the reader's attention**; tone, register and lexis all right; no blurring of ideas | Conventions used **reasonably**; simple, straightforward ideas; tone/genre may slip (a report that reads like a story) |
| **Organisation** | Planned, paragraphed, linked? | **Well-organised and coherent**, a **variety** of cohesive devices with good effect, paragraphs, intro + conclusion (essay), apt punctuation | Generally organised; **simple connectors** (and, besides, also, so, because) plus a limited number of cohesive devices; paragraphing still decent |
| **Language** | Vocabulary and grammar range and control | Wide range incl. **less common lexis used appropriately**; **simple AND complex** grammar with **control and flexibility**; only occasional errors/slips | Everyday vocabulary, **occasional wrong use of less common words**; simple + some complex grammar with good control; errors noticeable but **do not impede** meaning |
Part 1 is pitched lower (A2): high = basic vocabulary and simple grammar used **accurately**, full marks possible; average = simple ideas, high-frequency connectors, **errors may impede meaning at times**.

## What a high-scoring answer does (patterns across all the high samples)
1. **Hits every content point in its own clear sentence(s)**, in the order of the question, and says each one directly (name the thing, say where it came from, give a reason, give an opinion).
2. **Uses the right conventions** for the genre (see below), so the reader never doubts what kind of text it is.
3. **Plans paragraphs**: introduction, body, conclusion for an essay; an opening, build-up and resolution for a story; headings for a report.
4. **Mixes its connectors**, not just "and/so/because": *Firstly, On top of that, Besides, Since, Furthermore, Moreover, Last but not least, To illustrate, This is because, However, Therefore, Subsequently, Not only that, In conclusion*, and relative words (*who, which, that, when, after, due to*).
5. **Sprinkles less common lexis, appropriately**, not showy: *hectic lifestyle, sentimental value, heirloom, fixation, hoarder, simultaneously, consequences, financial struggle, a barrage of insults, silence filled the café, exploded, resonates, subtle, durable, vibrant atmosphere, sense of belonging, instill compassion and empathy, indispensable cornerstone*.
6. **Controls grammar with flexibility**: relative clauses, *not only... but also*, *rather than*, participle phrases, conditionals; the odd slip is fine.
7. **Ends the idea**: the essay concludes; the story resolves the conflict; the report gives what was learned.

## Per part: what "good" looks like and what loses marks

### Part 1: email / short message (A2, about 80 words)
- **Good:** appropriate opening ("Hello Siti," / "It's good to hear from you") and closing ("I hope my suggestion helps you", "See you soon"); informal, friendly tone; answers **all** the points in the prompt email (e.g. which movie, what kind, why they'd like it); connectors like *Firstly, On top of that, since, who, Besides*; simple, accurate sentences; basic words used well (*glad, hear, perfect, enjoy*). Short is fine.
- **Average:** all points present but ideas simple, connected by *and / besides / also / so*; correct email format; some errors that sometimes **blur the meaning** (e.g. "It is because the movie is on trending in the netflix").
- **Tip from the examiners:** read the rubric, find the content points and the target reader, use an informal tone, write short clear messages.

### Part 2: guided essay (B1, 125-150 words). Question seen: "something I cannot live without"
Content points: (1) name one important thing and where you got it, (2) why it is valuable, (3) are people too focused on having many things? Give reasons for the opinion.
- **Good (high):**
  - Opens by naming the thing with a hook: "One essential thing that I carry with me daily is my smartphone. With the hectic lifestyle... my phone would be the last thing left behind."
  - Says where it came from: "I got my phone from my father as a gift for managing to score well..."
  - Reason: "valuable to me since it stores all my data, memorable photos... the web browser which is efficient for me as a student."
  - **Gives a real opinion on the fourth point:** "Nowadays, I believe that people are too focused on having many things, precisely to please or show it off. Some may consider it a fixation, but... buying less stuff and focusing more on the necessities could benefit them financially and mentally."
  - Intro that sets the topic ("In life, each individual holds an item that means a great deal to them"), a conclusion that ties up, varied linkers (*However, because, in my daily life, such as, and so forth, when*).
- **Average:** the content is relevant but there are **omissions**; the classic loss is **writing about the key word but not answering the point** (the sample mentioned "people are too focused on having many things" then talked about protein food / why the phone is important: marked as irrelevant). Simple connectors (*Besides, because, that, wherever, when*), basic vocabulary (*school, homework, give, study*), simple grammar with noticeable errors ("the motorcycle give me some advantage").
- **Tip:** make the opinion point its own clear paragraph that directly answers the question as asked.

### Part 3 Q3: story (B2, 200-250 words). Question seen: start with "Hani almost spilled her coffee when she saw...", include what Hani felt and how the meeting fixed an old conflict
- **Good:** **starts with the exact opening sentence given**; says what the character **felt** (shy, sad, thought of leaving but stayed); shows **how the meeting fixed the conflict** (talk, apology, becoming friends again); story tone (past tense, narrative, dialogue, scene); cohesive devices *so, and, which, how, after*; vivid lexis (*final straw, exploded, exchanged a barrage of insults, silence filled the café*); dialogue; a few slips (*didn't dares, chasina*) are tolerated.
- **Average:** may **not start with the given opening line** (one sample started "...when she saw kitten running"), **omits a content point** (no resolution / no feelings), simple story told in simple sentences, discourse markers *Now, For example, Not only that, Moreover*, everyday vocabulary with an occasional misused advanced word (*confess*).

### Part 3 Q4: report (B2). Question seen: community project to clean an elderly person's house; answer WHEN, HOW MANY involved and WHAT you did, WHAT everyone learned
- **Good:** proper **report conventions**: **To / From / Date / Subject**, an **Introduction** stating the purpose ("This report outlines the date and time, participants..."), **headings** (e.g. *Lessons learnt by Students*), formal impersonal register (*first of all, next, furthermore, lastly, due to, which*), the **date, the number of people, each task and the lesson learned**, plus a recommendation-style close; lexis like *enthusiasm, high hopes, transform, installed, vibrant atmosphere, sense of belonging, social accountability, instill compassion and empathy*; grammar variety (*not merely... but an indispensable cornerstone*).
- **Average:** **tone and genre slip into a story or personal narrative** ("It was a cloudless morning, a wonderful start to the school holidays", "killing two birds with one stone", no To/From/Subject), a missing point (often "what everyone learned"), a **less common word used wrongly** ("voluntarism"), grammar errors ("There is a 15 students", "who is in needs of help") that do not block meaning. **The most common weakness in this question was not using the tone of a report.**

### Part 3 Q5: article (B2). Question seen: Teenagers and fashion: what influences your choices? comfort or style? how do you express yourself through fashion?
- **Good:** **title and byline** ("Teenagers and Fashion: An Article, by ..."), catchy opening ("it is undeniable that..."), answers **all three** questions with reasons and examples (influence: social media, Y2K, streetwear; comfort vs style with an illustration like high heels causing sore feet; self-expression: graphic T-shirts about the games I play, "resonates with my vibe"), connectors *This is because, To illustrate, it cannot be denied that, Furthermore, Moving on, On the other hand, To top it all off, Clearly*, and less common lexis (*resonates, literally, durable, accessory, recognise, subtle, hindrance, allure*).
- **Average:** one point **does not come through** (a typical miss: talking about a specific fashion/influencer or being "practical" and never saying **how you express yourself** through fashion), simple connectors (*Furthermore, instead of, because, but, Besides*), occasional misused phrase ("to deepen their wanted"). Errors do not block meaning.
- **Examiner note:** many candidates only described a specific fashion and forgot to answer **how they express themselves through fashion**.

## Rules for Claude when Gary gives a question and wants an essay "like that"
1. **List the content points first** (every bullet, every sub-question) and write one clear, relevant sentence group for each, in order. Never mention the key word without answering the point.
2. **Match the genre exactly:** email = greeting + friendly tone + closing; essay = intro / body / conclusion with the opinion stated; story = given opening line word for word, feelings, dialogue, resolution; report = To/From/Date/Subject, Introduction, headed sections, formal impersonal tone, no story language; article = title + byline, direct address, opinions with reasons and examples.
3. **Hit the word count** (80 / 125-150 / 200-250).
4. **Vary linkers** (at least 6 to 8 different ones, not just *and, so, because, also*) and use paragraphs.
5. **Language mix:** mostly clear everyday vocabulary used **correctly**, plus a handful (4 to 8) of less common words that fit naturally; simple + complex grammar (relative clause, *not only... but also*, *although*, participle). Prefer correct and natural over fancy.
6. **Write in a believable Form 5 voice** (the high samples are student writing, not literary prose).
7. **When marking:** score each criterion separately, name the exact content point missed, the genre slip, the connector range and the language level with quoted evidence; do not deduct for occasional slips that do not impede meaning (same error-vs-mistake logic as the speaking AI).

## Explain every mark (Gary, 8 Nov 2026): applies to the essay-marking AI and the speaking AI
Never give a number without telling the student why. For EACH criterion of an essay, output this:
```
Content: 4/5
Why 4: what earned it, quoting the student's own words (e.g. "You answered all 3 points; your reason 'it stores my photos' is clear").
Why not 5: the one thing missing against the band-5 descriptor (e.g. "Point 3 only says 'people spend too much' and gives no reason").
To reach 5: one concrete fix, ideally a rewritten sentence of the student's own.
```
- Justify against the band descriptors in the criteria table (High = 5, Average = 3, between = 4), not against a feeling. If every criterion comes out the same number, re-check each one.
- Quote real words from the essay; never invent quotes. Name the exact content point missed, the genre slip, the connectors used, the less common words used or missing.
- Occasional slips that do not impede meaning are not deducted (mistake vs error: only a repeated pattern is an error).
- End with the total /20, the single biggest thing that would add the most marks, and one strength to keep.
- The speaking AI does the same with "Band X because / Not Band Y because" (`comment`) plus "To reach Band Y" (`next_band`), shown on each scale card.

## Examiner suggestions (summary, from the KMJ)
- **Students:** read and understand the rubric; identify the content points and the target reader; plan intro-body-conclusion; fulfil the question; use a variety of cohesive devices; use the right tone and format; **write the question number for Part 3**; proofread; be careful with complex grammar and uncommon words; read widely to build vocabulary.
- **Teachers:** teach planning (mind maps); cohesive devices; complex grammatical forms; coherent essay strategies; the conventions of each genre; express opinions; grammar and spelling practice; use the KMJ as the reference.
